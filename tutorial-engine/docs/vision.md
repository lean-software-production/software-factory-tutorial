# Tutorial engine vision

The tutorial engine is the backbone of our self-directed courses: it presents an authored curriculum
with the editorial craft of a great New York Times or Guardian feature — scrollytelling, meaningful
visuals ahead of words, typography that earns a wow without reaching for a gimmick — and carries the
learner from meeting a concept, to practising it, to reflecting on it. Its agent is what makes that
workbook alive: a tutor that coaches through mistakes and misapprehensions and gives real feedback on
reflection, while doing as little as possible — never critical, never impatient, never spoiling the
answer, never questioning a learner who just wants one, and never making anybody say twice where they
are. A learner should close the laptop confident, satisfied, delighted, and hungry for the next lesson.

## Stakeholders

**Students** are experts paying a premium price, studying in their own evenings between live
sessions. A lesson has to be worth one sitting and easy to come back to, guiding them from concept to
practice to reflection. Many arrive sceptical that AI can build good software, so the workbook itself
has to be the counter-argument: quality they can feel.

**Authors** are domain experts who design the outcomes, concepts, exercises and reflection questions,
and work with an agent to design the visuals. They need a fast loop: develop, edit, walk it as a
learner would, refine.

**Facilitators** are head coaches. They see where each learner has reached and can ask a learner's
tutor how they are getting on and where they struggled. The patterns across a cohort are feedback to
the author about which lesson needs work.
